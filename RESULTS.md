# Results

Current code calls the historical "Fractional VQE" method a **variational
quantum allocator**: circuit expectations parameterise weights and the portfolio
objective is evaluated classically. Historical tables, figures, and manifests
below retain their original labels, data, and source provenance. The corrected
smoothed normalisation changes the allocation map, so new runs must use fresh
output directories.

This page separates deterministic synthetic examples from real market-data examples. The plots are committed artifacts generated by the notebook clients, so the documentation build stays deterministic and does not depend on live notebook execution or market-data downloads.

These examples do not establish quantum advantage. The [quantum advantage research protocol](QUANTUM_ADVANTAGE.md) defines prospective quality targets, budgets, and decision rules for that investigation; the existing tables have not been evaluated under that protocol.

## How to Read the Tables

The committed tables below use the historical schema: binary `feasible_rate` measures sample feasibility. Newly regenerated comparisons independently validate returned portfolios, use `feasible_rate` for returned-portfolio feasibility, and report sample feasibility separately as `sample_feasible_rate`. They also include a shared `problem_id`, and trial rows separate original objective scores from `penalized_energy`. See [shared problem definitions and evaluation](USAGE.md#shared-problem-definitions-and-evaluation).

For binary selection methods, lower objective is better for a fixed dataset, risk-aversion value, penalty, and cardinality. For fractional allocation methods, lower simplex objective is better at the same risk-aversion value.

Generated comparison tables include `objective_family` and `reported_weighting` columns because binary QUBO objectives and fractional simplex objectives are different mathematical objectives. In generated comparison rows, binary return and risk are reported from equal-weight selected portfolios (`x / K`) so risk-return plots are comparable with simplex allocations, while the binary objective remains the true QUBO objective evaluated on the raw bitstring `x`.

The real-data tables report values already saved in notebook outputs. Exact real-data classical optimizer rows should be regenerated together with the market-data notebooks because adjusted prices and shrinkage estimates are provider-dependent.

## Small-Instance Validation Pilot

The [validation runner](scripts/run_small_instance_validation.py) completed 198 trials across 4, 6 and 8 securities, with three independent synthetic factor-model instances per size and three solver seeds per stochastic method. Binary cardinality is half the universe size. Quantum methods use 25 optimisation steps on a noiseless simulator; the binary methods sample 512 candidates. Fractional allocation is a separate control track. See the [configuration and reproduction instructions](experiments/README.md) and [full report](results/small_instance_validation/report.md).

All 198 returned portfolios passed independent feasibility checks. No solver exceptions or reference contradictions were recorded. The target is a feasible normalised objective gap of at most 0.001 against the corresponding reference, using the scale defined in the research protocol.

| Method | Target hit: 4 securities | 6 securities | 8 securities |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Classical methods (each, in its own track) | 100% | 100% | 100% |
| Binary VQE | 66.7% | 33.3% | 0% |
| QAOA X | 100% | 100% | 100% |
| QAOA XY | 66.7% | 66.7% | 0% |
| Fractional VQE | 33.3% | 11.1% | 33.3% |

QAOA X returned the exact binary optimum in every trial, but the classical best-of-512 uniformly sampled feasible subsets baseline also did so. These small search spaces therefore do not establish a benefit from quantum parameter optimisation. All classical comparators had shorter mean runtimes than the corresponding quantum simulator methods in this run. These timings describe the tested implementation and machine, not QPU performance.

The pilot validates the comparison pipeline and exposes reliability differences for these settings. It has no matched end-to-end time budgets, uncertainty estimates or held-out tuning study, and does not establish quantum advantage or a scaling law. In particular, the three tested sizes do not justify extrapolating a crossover. Frozen inputs, references, software/source metadata, raw candidates and trial-level scores are saved under `results/small_instance_validation/`.

### Effect of Training on Binary Circuit Distributions

The [before/after diagnostic](experiments/README.md#before-and-after-training-diagnostic) compares each full circuit at its initial random parameters and after 25 Adam updates, using the same nine frozen binary problems and three initialisation seeds. All 81 pairs completed without errors. The measure below is the exact **single-draw probability** of a feasible portfolio within the normalised quality target (0.001), not the chance of finding one after 512 samples. No sampling or Top-K repair is used in this diagnostic.

| Securities | Method | Initial target probability | Trained target probability | Improved / worsened pairs |
| ---: | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 4 | Binary VQE | 6.124% | 26.65% | 4 / 5 |
| 4 | QAOA X | 6.346% | 11.64% | 8 / 1 |
| 4 | QAOA XY | 24.64% | 41.17% | 5 / 1 |
| 6 | Binary VQE | 0.9142% | 18.53% | 4 / 5 |
| 6 | QAOA X | 2.075% | 4.312% | 8 / 1 |
| 6 | QAOA XY | 4.226% | 35.55% | 5 / 1 |
| 8 | Binary VQE | 0.5765% | 0.003692% | 0 / 9 |
| 8 | QAOA X | 0.5499% | 1.090% | 9 / 0 |
| 8 | QAOA XY | approximately zero | approximately zero | 0 / 0 |

Means average seeds within each instance, then give instances equal weight. Improved/worsened counts use a numerical deadband of `1e-12` probability; remaining pairs are unchanged within that tolerance. Numerically negligible QAOA XY target mass at eight securities is below that deadband before and after training.

Training improves mean target probability at four and six securities for all three methods, but Binary VQE worsens in five of nine runs at each of those sizes: a few large gains raise the mean. At eight securities, Binary VQE's mean feasibility rises from 30.52% to 97.22%, while its target probability falls in every run. QAOA X improves unconditional target probability in 25 of 27 pairs; its mean target probability conditional on feasibility nevertheless decreases at four and six securities. Better constraint satisfaction and better selection among feasible portfolios are distinct outcomes.

These observations apply to the tested settings and small development-instance sample. They do not establish an advantage over classical methods, statistical significance, or a scaling law. Fractional allocation is outside this binary sampling diagnostic. The [full report](results/training_diagnostic/report.md), [paired metrics](results/training_diagnostic/pairs.csv), checkpointed raw distributions and [plot](results/training_diagnostic/training_probability.png) are saved separately from the original validation artifacts.

### Sampling Allowance: 16, 64 and 512 Draws

The [sampling allowance diagnostic](experiments/README.md#sampling-allowance-diagnostic) reuses all 81 initial/trained pairs. For each run it calculates `1 - (1 - p)^m` before averaging across seeds and instances. It also evaluates uniform sampling of feasible exact-K subsets with replacement, once per instance and allowance. No training or new sampling occurs. Success requires at least one raw feasible target-quality portfolio; infeasible circuit samples consume draws, and no Top-K repair is applied.

At eight securities, the expected success probabilities are:

| Distribution | 16 draws | 64 draws | 512 draws |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Binary VQE, initial | 8.446% | 26.15% | 61.23% |
| Binary VQE, trained | 0.05893% | 0.2339% | 1.740% |
| QAOA X, initial | 8.344% | 28.48% | 87.15% |
| QAOA X, trained | 15.92% | 48.79% | 98.03% |
| QAOA XY, initial or trained | approximately zero | approximately zero | approximately zero |
| Classical uniform feasible | 26.08% | 68.24% | 99.96% |

Increasing the allowance to 512 makes QAOA X highly reliable in this small pilot, but the classical baseline matches or exceeds every original fixed-setting circuit's mean success probability at every tested size and allowance. No sampling-reliability advantage is observed for those circuits and settings.

The before/after result depends on the allowance: Binary VQE at four and six securities has a higher mean single-draw target probability after training, yet its expected 512-draw success falls from 89.74% to 49.96% and from 68.85% to 34.05%, respectively. A few large single-draw gains coexist with runs where target probability becomes tiny. Evaluating the nonlinear success formula on the mean single-draw probability would hide that heterogeneity.

These analytical probabilities are not the observed fractions in the earlier finite-sample pilot. The calculation assumes independent fixed-distribution draws with replacement, excludes training/execution costs and does not establish a computational or quantum advantage. Tables may round probabilities near certainty to 100%; CSVs retain full precision. See the [full report and curves](results/sampling_allowance/report.md), [per-run allowance results](results/sampling_allowance/pairs.csv) and [summary](results/sampling_allowance/summary.csv).

### Training Effort: 0, 25 and 100 Steps

The [training-effort study](experiments/README.md#training-effort-development-study) completed 81 uninterrupted Adam trajectories and evaluated 243 checkpoint distributions. All initial and step-25 parameters/probabilities reproduced the saved diagnostic within numerical tolerance. Optimiser moments were preserved through step 100; distribution evaluation occurred after training, outside the cumulative optimisation timer. No trajectory errors occurred.

The effect of extending training from 25 to 100 steps on expected success within 512 raw draws was:

| Securities | Method | 25 steps | 100 steps |
| ---: | --- | ---: | ---: |
| 4 | Binary VQE | 49.96% | 66.67% |
| 4 | QAOA X | approximately 100% | approximately 100% |
| 4 | QAOA XY | 66.67% | 66.67% |
| 6 | Binary VQE | 34.05% | 22.22% |
| 6 | QAOA X | approximately 100% | approximately 100% |
| 6 | QAOA XY | 66.67% | 66.67% |
| 8 | Binary VQE | 1.740% | <0.00001% |
| 8 | QAOA X | 98.03% | 98.42% |
| 8 | QAOA XY | approximately zero | approximately zero |

More training does not consistently improve reliability under these settings. At six securities, Binary VQE's mean single-draw target probability increases from 18.53% to 22.21%, while 512-draw success decreases: two runs improve and seven worsen between checkpoints. At eight securities, all nine Binary VQE runs worsen, despite mean feasibility approaching 100%. QAOA XY's negligible eight-security target probability persists.

For eight securities, QAOA X's extra 75 steps improve expected 512-draw success by about 0.391 percentage points, while measured mean cumulative optimiser time rises from 2.11 to 8.17 seconds. Uniform feasible classical sampling from the prior allowance analysis reaches 99.96% at the same draw count, excluding execution/training costs. This does not establish quantum advantage or a fixed-time comparison.

The [full report](results/training_effort/report.md) includes single-draw quality, feasibility, all three sampling allowances and cumulative times. [Paired plots](results/training_effort/training_pairs.png) show individual runs, and [summary plots](results/training_effort/training_effort.png) show means. Raw parameters/distributions, input/source hashes and paired changes are retained. This development study uses the existing small instance set; settings chosen from it need fresh held-out evaluation.

### Eight-Security Failure Diagnostics

The [failure study](experiments/README.md#eight-security-failure-diagnostics) completed 81 depth/penalty variants on the three frozen eight-security instances and three seeds: 18 verified baseline trajectories were reused and 63 new trajectories were trained. All use the inherited 100-step effort and checkpoints at 0, 25 and 100. No errors occurred. Exact support, penalty thresholds, raw distributions, modal portfolios, gradients and illustrative swap-path barriers are retained with input/source hashes.

**XY QAOA has a structural depth-1 obstruction.** From its initial `11110000` portfolio, the ordered mixer can reach only `11110000`, `11101000`, `01110001` and `01101001` at depth 1. Exact integer Laurent-polynomial amplitudes establish that support for all beta angles. None of the target portfolios in any of the three instances is in that set. The first cost angle contributes only a global phase; extra optimisation cannot overcome this depth-1 exclusion.

At depths 2 and 3, gate-path support upper bounds cover 66 and 70 of the 70 feasible bitstrings, respectively. These are bounds rather than exact higher-depth reachability claims. Actual initial distributions have positive target mass at the higher depths, demonstrating that targets can be reached, but the 100-step training sharply reduces it. At depth 3, expected 512-draw success is only 1.107% after training. Removing the depth-1 obstruction alone does not produce reliable optimisation under the tested settings.

**Binary VQE has sufficient basis-state expressivity at both depths.** A direct RY parameter construction represents every basis portfolio, including the targets. The baseline penalty of 5 also exceeds all three exhaustive strict-sufficiency thresholds: approximately 0.02923, 0.06385 and 0.009771. Thus the baseline failure is not explained by unrepresentable target states or an infeasible global penalised optimum.

At 100 steps, the VQE sensitivity results are:

| Depth | Alpha | Mean feasibility | Expected success: 512 draws |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2 | 0.05 | 88.89% | 44.45% |
| 2 | 0.5 | approximately 100% | approximately zero |
| 2 | 5 | approximately 100% | approximately zero |
| 3 | 0.05 | 66.71% | 65.54% |
| 3 | 0.5 | approximately 100% | 11.11% |
| 3 | 5 | approximately 100% | approximately zero |

Alpha 0.05 is below the feasibility-sufficiency threshold for instance 202, where its penalised ground state is infeasible. Its improved sampling results are not a validated universal penalty choice. Quality always scores the original feasible objective; infeasible samples consume draws. All nine baseline VQE modal portfolios are feasible yet admit an improving one-for-one swap. Their measured gradients and barriers along a specified product-state swap path are diagnostics, not certificates of convergence, local minima or barriers along all possible entangled paths.

The [support figure](results/failure_diagnostics/xy_support.png), [sensitivity figure](results/failure_diagnostics/failure_sensitivity.png), [VQE convergence figure](results/failure_diagnostics/vqe_convergence.png) and [full report](results/failure_diagnostics/report.md) separate structural exclusion, penalty adequacy and observed training behaviour. These development results do not establish a scaling law or quantum advantage, and settings chosen from this grid require fresh held-out evaluation.

## v0.3.1 solver reliability and deadline pilots

The [solver reliability study](results/solver_reliability/report.md) compares
reference-free penalty selection, normalization, restarts, best-iterate selection,
and learning-rate decay for Binary VQE, plus basis/Dicke initial states and
ring/complete XY networks. Every variant receives the same total Adam updates;
wall-clock time and circuit-evaluation counts are not equalized. Exact target
probabilities are evaluated after training on the frozen original objectives.

64 draws from 0% to 90.96%; uniform exact-K sampling reaches 68.24%. The combined
Binary VQE restart strategy reaches 11.89%, versus 0.19% for its legacy control.

improvement or held-out performance.

## Fresh-instance deadline comparison

The [fresh-instance report](results/fresh_instance_budget/report.md) contains
594 completed trials across 4, 6, and 8 assets, three fresh factor-model instances
per size (seeds 707, 808, and 909), two run seeds, and independent 1/10/60-second
allowances. Settings were fixed before evaluating these inputs. The runner rejects
overlap with the declared development manifests and saves their hashes. See the
[frozen configuration](experiments/fresh_instance_budget.json) and
[reproduction instructions](experiments/README.md#fresh-instance-deadline-comparison).

The central comparison uses identical production parameter initializations for
trained and untrained Dicke-ring XY QAOA. The trained variant publishes candidates
during 24 Adam updates, then repeatedly samples the final parameters for its
remaining allowance. The untrained control repeatedly samples its initial
parameters with zero updates. Both execute actual 64-shot circuit batches.
Sampling can stop once every feasible portfolio has actually been observed,
using only the domain size rather than a reference target. Other trained
comparators retain their declared finite update caps and ordinary final sampling.

At the primary **10-second allowance**, binary target-hit rates are:

| Binary method/variant | 4 assets | 6 assets | 8 assets |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Binary VQE, legacy | 50.0% | 33.3% | 16.7% |
| Binary VQE, improved | 83.3% | 100.0% | 33.3% |
| QAOA X | 100.0% | 100.0% | 100.0% |
| XY QAOA, basis/ring | 33.3% | 0.0% | 66.7% |
| XY QAOA, Dicke/ring, trained | 100.0% | 100.0% | 100.0% |
| XY QAOA, Dicke/ring, untrained | 100.0% | 100.0% | 100.0% |
| HiGHS, swap search, uniform exact-K (each) | 100.0% | 100.0% | 100.0% |

Each cell contains six runs, with instances weighted equally. Trained and
untrained Dicke-ring circuits and all three classical controls return the exact
binary optimum in every 10-second run. This endpoint therefore shows no quality
or target-hit benefit from training at these sizes. These are elapsed-time
outcomes, distinct from the historical analytical success probabilities at a
fixed 64-draw allowance. Across the 18 primary-budget runs per method, mean
observed first-target times are 2.01 seconds for trained Dicke-ring, 1.87 seconds
for untrained Dicke-ring, and between 1.74 and 1.83 seconds for the three classical
controls. These are descriptive implementation timings, without uncertainty
estimates or a hardware speed claim.

In the separate fractional control track, Fractional VQE hits its own target in
50.0%, 16.7%, and 33.3% of 10-second runs at 4, 6, and 8 assets, respectively;
OSQP reaches 100.0% at each size. Binary and fractional objectives are not pooled.

All 198 one-second runs time out during startup, before worker setup is observed.
Where worker entry is observed, startup averages 1.77 seconds on this environment
(range 1.47 to 3.63 seconds). Every 10/60-second run returns a feasible portfolio.
Four runs reach their deadline after returning an incumbent; these remain in the
denominators with their delivered portfolios. No solver errors or reference
contradictions occur. Maximum observed cleanup overrun is 0.0344 seconds and does
not extend candidate eligibility. Parent validation must finish before the
deadline for a portfolio to count.

[Timing records](results/fresh_instance_budget/timings.csv) separate startup,
setup, optimization, candidate processing, delivery, parent validation, cleanup,
and interrupted phases. Delivery and validation overlap worker work and must not
be added to the worker phase totals. [Paired records](results/fresh_instance_budget/pairs.csv)
retain matched instance/seed/budget outcomes and failed returns; conditional
quality differences require two feasible outputs. The saved empirical T90 uses
only two repeats per instance and is a coarse descriptive statistic. This small
fresh-instance check has no confidence intervals, formal held-out tuning study,
scaling conclusion, or quantum-advantage claim. Historical evidence is preserved.
